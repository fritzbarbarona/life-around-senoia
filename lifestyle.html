Freedom Worth Celebrating

Every July, we're reminded how blessed we are to call America home — and this year, as the country marked its 250th birthday, that gratitude felt even more significant. Here in Senoia, neighbors gathered, fireworks lit up the night sky, and flags waved proudly from front porches all across downtown.

Among all the freedoms worth celebrating, one is especially easy to overlook: the freedom to worship. Across our community each week, church doors open without fear. It's a reminder that freedom is never something to assume — it's a gift, purchased through sacrifice and worthy of both our gratitude and our remembrance.

Issue 1

Community: Senoia's True Secret Sauce

Ask anyone what they love about Senoia and you'll almost always hear the same answer: the community. Yes, there's the small-town charm, the Hollywood history, the local shops, parks, and trails — but Senoia's heart runs deeper than those surface-level treasures.

What makes Senoia so special is the sense of belonging it cultivates. Neighbors care, strangers are welcomed, and differences are respected. It doesn't require uniformity, only unity.

Local History

Keeping Senoia's Story Alive

Senoia Area History Museum
6 Couch Street

The Senoia Area Historical Society exists to secure, preserve, and promote the social and cultural history of our community — but at its heart, it isn't just about dates and documents. It's about the people who built the town, the places that shaped it, and the volunteers who make sure those stories aren't lost to time.

  • 1976 A group of neighbors celebrates the Bicentennial — $55.89 left over becomes the Society's seed money.
  • 1980 The Senoia Area Historical Society officially incorporates.
  • 1989 Senoia's Historic District is added to the National Register of Historic Places.
  • 1990 The Society purchases the historic 1870s Carmichael home at 6 Couch Street.
  • 2010 The Senoia Area History Museum opens its doors on July 18.

Open weekends · senoiahistory.com

Senoia Sunrise

Some cocktails are made for celebrations. Others are made for slow summer afternoons — the Senoia Sunrise is both. It borrows the bright citrus of a classic mimosa and pairs it with the smooth warmth of bourbon for something crisp, refreshing, and surprisingly easy to sip. Cheers!

Ingredients

  • 2 oz Doc Brown's Day Swigger Southern Ember Whiskey
  • 3 oz fresh orange juice
  • Splash of fresh lime juice
  • Sparkling water, to top
  • Fresh peach slice, for garnish

Instructions

  1. Fill a cocktail shaker with ice.
  2. Add the whiskey, orange juice, and lime juice. Shake well until chilled.
  3. Fill a rocks glass with fresh ice and strain the cocktail into the glass.
  4. Top with sparkling water.
  5. Garnish with a fresh peach slice.

Please enjoy responsibly. Must be 21+ to consume alcohol.
