The Brewington Family
Issue 6
Issue 6 · Featured Family

Making Room at the Table

Spend a few minutes with Darrin, Ashley, and their nearly two-year-old daughter Nayeli, and their warmth is contagious. Both Georgia natives, they grew up in Fayetteville, married in 2019, and met working at Chick-fil-A — starting on the very same day. They chose Senoia because it placed them right between both sides of their family.

Darrin's path led from Worship Pastor at New Vision Church to Newnan City Church, where the Coweta tornado reshaped how he saw ministry — the most meaningful moments happening in living rooms and around dinner tables. Today he's planting Abide Church in Senoia, with a soft launch this August and an official launch on January 10, 2027, while Ashley serves patients as an oncology nurse. The belief behind it all: everyone deserves to know there's a place for them.

"Community isn't something you stumble upon — it's something you intentionally create."
The Bartels Family
Issue 5
Issue 5 · Featured Family

Built to Belong

When Kevin and Kari Bartels moved from Texas four years ago, they didn't just relocate — they stepped into the rhythm of a town built on connection and kindness. You might find Kari welcoming visitors at the Welcome Center, or the couple sharing a meal with friends at Nic & Norman's or Borgo Italia.

Their story hasn't been without hard seasons, but instead of drifting apart they rebuilt with intention through Re|Engage, a marriage ministry they'll soon help pilot at ONE Church in Fayetteville — turning what once restored their own marriage into something they can offer others.

"You do not have to be born here to belong here."
The Jenkins Family

The Jenkins Family Is All In

Stephen and Katelyn Jenkins are ALL IN on life. They show up in big ways — for each other, for their son, for their friends, for their church, and for this community they proudly call home.

Since moving to Senoia from Thomaston in 2018, they haven't just lived here — they've leaned in, drawn by the town's charm and a search for a next chapter closer to family and easier airport access.

The Crook Family

Issue 3 · Featured Family

A Family Whose Values Helped Shape a Town — and Still Do

Some families leave a mark on a town. Others become part of its foundation. In Senoia, the Crook family is the latter — a name woven quietly but deeply into the character of the community.

For Ellis Crook, those values weren't abstract ideas. They were a way of life, passed down from his father Arry Lee: hard work, integrity, faith, and service.

Joe & Dawn McGee
Issue 2
Issue 2 · Featured Family

The Heart Behind Santa Joe

For Joe and Dawn McGee, the red Santa suit represents something deeper than the season — faith, redemption, and turning pain into light for others. Their love story began 40 years ago at a church softball game; together they raised three children and are now "Ahdah and Nana" to four grandchildren.

Joe's calling as "Santa Joe" grew out of loss — the passing of his brother David, the original Santa in the family, and his own cancer diagnosis. From it came the Santa David Children's Fund, which has donated nearly $600,000 to support children battling cancer at CHOA and UFHPTI.

"It's not about the suit. It's about Jesus."

Shocking the World, One Race at a Time — Milo Stupski

At first glance, nine-year-old Milo Stupski might seem a little quiet — until you put him on a dirt bike. Known on the circuit as the "Hole Shot King," Milo competes in the Grand National Cross Country (GNCC) Series and currently sits ranked No. 3 in the nation in the Micro-E division. With six victories and counting, he lives by the three words written across his handlebars: Shock the World. His advice to other kids? "Don't give up. Be brave."

Doc Brown Farm & Distillers
Issue 6
Issue 6 · Feature

Hidden in Plain Sight

Tucked behind a line of trees just outside Senoia sits Doc Brown Farm & Distillers — but ask Amy Brown, her son Dan Williams, and Paige Dockweiler, and they'll tell you it isn't really a story about whiskey. It's about family, stewardship, and the connection between people and the land.

On a 20-acre farmhouse property with 100 leased acres across the road, they grow heritage grains like Jimmy Red Corn and Abruzzi rye for their award-winning spirits, and host farm tours throughout the year — all guided by a simple philosophy.

"Be kind to the earth and kind to each other."
Senoia Ladies Mahjong Group
Issue 6
Issue 6 · Feature

Finding Community Through Mahjong

After falling in love with the game, Becky Egecrone started the Senoia Ladies Mahjong Group just to find a few playing partners. She never imagined it would grow to more than 450 members, with games two or three times a week hosted by local businesses like Lisa's Creperie and 404 Celsius.

Ask Becky why it took off, though, and the answer has little to do with the game itself — and everything to do with people stepping away from their screens to sit across from one another.

"There's always a spot for you at a Mahjong table."
